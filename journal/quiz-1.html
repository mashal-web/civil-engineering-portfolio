Number systems & unit conversions.

1. Number systems

A number system represents values using a defined set of digits. Its base tells you how many digit symbols it uses.

SystemBaseAllowed digits
Binary20, 1
Octal80–7
Decimal100–9
Hexadecimal160–9 and A–F

In hexadecimal, A = 10, B = 11, C = 12, D = 13, E = 14 and F = 15.

Binary to decimal

Multiply each bit by its power of 2, starting at 2⁰ on the right, then add the results.

Example: 1011₂ to decimal

1×2³ + 0×2² + 1×2¹ + 1×2⁰ = 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11₁₀.

Decimal to binary

Divide repeatedly by 2. Record each remainder and read the remainders from bottom to top.

DivisionQuotientRemainder
13 ÷ 261
6 ÷ 230
3 ÷ 211
1 ÷ 201

Read upward: 13₁₀ = 1101₂.

Binary to octal

Group the bits in sets of three, starting from the right. Add leading zeros to the left if needed. Convert each group to one octal digit.

Example: 110101₂ → 110 | 101 → 6 | 5 → 65₈.

Octal to binary

Replace each octal digit with its three-bit binary equivalent.

OctalBinaryOctalBinary
00004100
10015101
20106110
30117111

Example: 57₈ → 101 | 111 → 101111₂.

Binary to hexadecimal

Group bits in sets of four from the right. Convert each group to one hexadecimal digit.

Example: 11101101₂ → 1110 | 1101 → E | D → ED₁₆.

Hexadecimal to binary

Replace each hexadecimal digit with its four-bit binary equivalent.

Example: A5₁₆ → A = 1010 and 5 = 0101 → 10100101₂.

Decimal to octal

Divide repeatedly by 8, record the remainders and read them from bottom to top.

Example: 25 ÷ 8 = 3 remainder 1; 3 ÷ 8 = 0 remainder 3. Therefore, 25₁₀ = 31₈.

Decimal to hexadecimal

Divide repeatedly by 16 and read the remainders from bottom to top. Remainders 10–15 are written as A–F.

Example: 45 ÷ 16 = 2 remainder 13 (D); 2 ÷ 16 = 0 remainder 2. Therefore, 45₁₀ = 2D₁₆.

Grouping rule: Binary to octal uses groups of 3 bits. Binary to hexadecimal uses groups of 4 bits. Group from the right and pad only the leftmost group with zeros when necessary.

2. Binary arithmetic

Binary addition

OperationResult
0 + 00
0 + 11
1 + 01
1 + 110 (write 0, carry 1)

Example: 101₂ + 011₂ = 1000₂. Check in decimal: 5 + 3 = 8.

Another example: 1101₂ + 1011₂ = 11000₂. Check: 13 + 11 = 24.

Binary subtraction

OperationResult
0 − 00
1 − 01
1 − 10
0 − 1Borrow: 10₂ − 1 = 1

When borrowing one from the next column, the current column receives 10₂ (which equals decimal 2).

Multiply as in decimal, but use only binary digits. Shift each partial product one place left for each next position of the multiplier.

3. Unit conversions

Unit conversion changes the unit used to express a measurement, not the quantity itself.

Length

ConversionEquivalent
1 kilometre (km)1000 metres (m)
1 metre (m)100 centimetres (cm)
1 centimetre (cm)10 millimetres (mm)
1 metre (m)1000 millimetres (mm)
1 inch2.54 cm
1 foot12 inches
1 mileabout 1.609 km

Examples: 5 km × 1000 = 5000 m. 250 cm ÷ 100 = 2.5 m.

Mass

ConversionEquivalent
1 kilogram (kg)1000 grams (g)
1 gram (g)1000 milligrams (mg)
1 metric tonne1000 kg
1 pound (lb)about 0.453592 kg

Example: 3.5 kg × 1000 = 3500 g.

Time

Example: 2.5 hours × 60 = 150 minutes.

Area

ConversionEquivalent
1 m²10,000 cm²
1 km²1,000,000 m²
1 hectare (ha)10,000 m²
1 acreabout 4046.86 m²

Example: 4 m² × 100² = 40,000 cm².

Volume

ConversionEquivalent
1 m³1000 litres (L)
1 litre (L)1000 millilitres (mL)
1 cm³1 mL
1 m³1,000,000 cm³

Example: 2 m³ × 1000 = 2000 L.

Conversion direction: A larger unit to a smaller unit usually means multiply. A smaller unit to a larger unit usually means divide. Always use the correct conversion factor and check the units.

Important: squared and cubed units

For area, square the length conversion factor. For volume, cube it.

1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 100² = 10,000 cm², and 1 m³ = 100³ = 1,000,000 cm³.
