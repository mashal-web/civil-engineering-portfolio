LEARNING NOTE / 001

How to approach a new engineering concept.

A repeatable method for turning unfamiliar terminology into a clear explanation and a checked example.

1. Define the physical idea

Before memorizing equations, explain in plain language what the system does. Identify the objects involved, the quantities that matter and the question you are trying to answer.

2. Define quantities and units

Write down known values, unknown values and their units. Check that quantities use compatible units before substituting them into an equation.

3. State assumptions

Simplified examples depend on assumptions. Record what is being ignored and why the model may not represent every real-world condition.

4. Work through the method

Show the governing principle, equation, substitutions and intermediate steps. Keep symbols consistent and avoid skipping a step that affects the result.

5. Check the answer

Check dimensions, signs, magnitude and whether the answer makes physical sense. Where possible, verify it using a second method or a trusted course reference.

6. Record the source

Use relevant textbooks, course notes, standards or instructor-provided material. Record enough details to find the source again and distinguish sourced facts from personal interpretation.

This is a general study framework, not a substitute for discipline-specific methods, engineering standards or instructor guidance.
